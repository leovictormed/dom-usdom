Stage 659 — permanent liveness and economic DoS audit (2026-10-23)

Scope

Audited legitimate-fund liveness across USDT blacklist/freeze/confiscation,
Safe and verifier drift, pause/resume, permanent checkpoint halt, queue expiry,
permanent burn/request bindings and the deliberate absence of collateral sweep
or deposit refund.

Result

No additional contract defect was confirmed and no administrative escape hatch
was added.

Recoverable failures

- A temporary recipient blacklist rolls back execution before paid flags,
  pending accounting, bucket balance or token balances change.
- Queue expiry releases only the reservation. The permanent proved statement
  and burn/request pairing survive, and the exact debt can renew even after its
  original finality slot becomes old.
- Emergency pause invalidates the queue generation but not the permanent debt.
  After delayed resume, the same statement can be proved and queued again.
- Safe or verifier identity drift fails before value movement. Restoring the
  exact committed identity makes the existing/renewed debt executable.
- A failed token transfer or policy check reverts the complete payout and leaves
  the claim retryable.

Intentional permanent-risk boundaries

- Permanent USDT blacklisting of the signed recipient makes that exact payout
  unavailable. Redirecting it administratively would violate the native burn's
  authenticated recipient. A safe redirect would require a separately designed
  recipient authorization protocol, including contract-wallet signatures and
  replay protection; it cannot be inferred by this reserve.
- USDT confiscation that removes reserve collateral produces explicit
  insolvency. Minting accounting credit, paying from donations without backing,
  or adding a sweep does not restore one-to-one collateral.
- Permanent loss or destruction of the exact Safe/verifier identities can stop
  progress. An upgrade/rotation escape hatch would create new authority over
  the proof root or collateral and contradict the immutable production model.
- `haltPermanently()` is deliberately irreversible emergency containment for
  the checkpoint registry. It cannot be treated as ordinary recovery.
- Deposits have no refund because an accepted deposit is the authorization for
  native USDOM issuance. An Ethereum-only refund could coexist with issued
  USDOM and create unbacked supply.

These are explicit trust/availability risks for operations and user disclosure,
not hidden claims that zero risk is achievable. None can be removed locally
without changing the cross-chain consensus or authorization model.

Material regression

`testLegitimateDebtSurvivesBlacklistExpiryPauseAndVerifierDrift` follows one
authenticated debt through a temporary recipient blacklist, queue expiry and
discard, stale-finality exact renewal, emergency queue invalidation, delayed
resume, another exact renewal and verifier drift. Every failed attempt preserves
the debt. Restoring the verifier pays the original recipient exactly once and
clears pending accounting.
