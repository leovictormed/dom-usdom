Stage 618 — Solidity proof-domain separation audit (2026-10-23)

Scope

Audited the Solidity boundary joining `BurnIntent`, `FinalizedBurn`, reserve and
checkpoint configuration, RISC Zero adapters, Ethereum `chainid`, contract
addresses, DOM/bridge domains and the activation schedule commitment. No Rust,
guest or proof-launcher code was inspected or changed in this stage.

Result

No Solidity defect was confirmed.

- `BurnIntent` commits burn/request IDs, recipient, amount, DOM domain,
  deadline, Ethereum chain ID and the exact reserve address. It deliberately
  excludes fields that exist only after finalization. Although it does not
  repeat `activationScheduleCommitment`, the exact reserve address selects one
  immutable activation commitment, and the reserve configuration hash commits
  both that address and commitment.
- `FinalizedBurn` uses a distinct type hash and additionally commits height,
  block, state root, Beacon slot and the activation schedule commitment. The
  queue recomputes this hash using current `block.chainid` and `address(this)`.
- Checkpoint statements use their own versioned type hash and commit bridge
  domain, network/state versions, the complete predecessor and successor,
  confirmation work, Ethereum chain ID, exact registry address, activation
  schedule and `checkpointConfigHash`. The latter commits DOM chain identity,
  verifier identity and the ratified bootstrap/control plane.
- The burn and checkpoint RISC Zero adapters pin chain ID, underlying verifier
  address/code, selector, ImageID, journal schema and distinct acceptance
  capabilities. Callers require their exact capability, so the common raw
  32-byte journal representation does not merge the two protocols.
- Solidity does not directly call the checkpoint registry from the reserve.
  That relation is intentionally inside the immutable finalized-burn guest
  selected by its ImageID. Solidity authenticates that program and its exact
  public statement; correctness of the guest's registry/event verification is
  a separate non-Solidity proof boundary and was not inferred here.

Material regressions added

1. `testProofCannotReplayAcrossReserveDomainOrActivationDeployment` proves
   that one approved burn proof cannot queue on deployments with another
   activation schedule or DOM domain. It checks reserve configuration,
   finalized statement and native intent separation, then confirms failed
   replay attempts do not consume the valid original authorization.
2. `testCheckpointProofCannotReplayAcrossRegistryDomainOrActivationDeployment`
   proves rejection after changing Ethereum chain ID, registry deployment,
   bridge domain or activation schedule, then submits the same proof
   successfully only to the exact registry tuple for which it was approved.

Validation

- new replay regressions: 2/2 passed;
- reserve, registry and both adapter suites: 77/77 passed, including four
  1,024-run fuzz tests;
- full Solidity campaign: 150/150 passed across 14 suites, including three
  256-run/16,384-call invariant campaigns;
- lint with warnings denied for all four audited production contracts: passed;
- changed-test formatting and `git diff --check`: passed.

No commit, push, deployment, signature or funds operation was performed.
