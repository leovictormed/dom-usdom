ETAPA 1244 — Safe policy deployment-chain audit
Date: 2026-10-26

Scope
-----

Independent adversarial review of the Solidity reserve and checkpoint control
planes after the fixed-memory Safe decoder optimization. The review emphasized
malformed returndata, assembly canonicality, chain-ID drift, permanent deposit
closure and proved burn debt. No Rust, scripts, network, signing, deployment,
funds, commit or push operation was performed.

Finding
-------

The runtime Safe-policy predicate selected the mandatory pinned mainnet fallback
handler with `block.chainid == 1`. Both contracts already preserve their
deployment chain in an immutable and bind economic statements to it, but the
control-plane check used the current chain instead.

Consequently, after a mainnet chain-ID change or execution on a surviving fork
with a different chain ID, the reserve and checkpoint registry would continue
to recognize the immutable Safe proxy/singleton/owners/threshold/modules/guard
identity while silently omitting the fallback-handler identity and code-hash
check. Chain drift cannot be triggered by an ordinary transaction, but it must
not weaken an immutable production policy during a fork or coordinated network
transition involving real collateral.

Correction
----------

`USDOMProofReserve._safePolicyWithoutOwnersIntact()` and
`DOMCheckpointRegistry._safePolicyIntact()` now select production Safe policy
from `deploymentChainId`, never from the mutable execution chain ID. Constructor
checks continue to use `block.chainid`, because construction defines that
immutable deployment domain. New exposure and new checkpoint admission retain
their existing explicit chain-drift rejection; already proved reserve debt
remains payable as designed.

The test Safe gained exact adversarial `getOwners()` modes for:

- a 159-byte short response;
- a 161-byte trailing response;
- a one-megabyte return bomb;
- a wrong dynamic offset with exact total length;
- a wrong owner count with exact total length; and
- an owner word with dirty upper 96 bits.

The ProofReserve regression exercises every mode through the public health
predicate and a value path, then proves exact restoration. Reserve and Registry
regressions also change the current chain ID and demonstrate that the policy
class remains the class selected at deployment while threshold drift still
fails closed.

Debt and closure result
-----------------------

No new debt/closure defect was found. First finalized-burn proofs remain
admissible after permanent deposit closure, but only against unreserved credited
collateral and the immutable verifier/registry/deployment domain. Renewals do
not reserve debt twice. Expiry, pruning and pause invalidate queue leases without
reducing permanent proved debt. Settlement checks the exact burn/request/debt/
finality tuple before reducing the aggregate. Terminal shutdown requires all
collateral released, zero proved debt, an empty queue, pinned registry identity,
issuance closure and equal authenticated issued/burned/deposited totals.

Release impact
--------------

rematerialized once concurrent source work is stable. The correction preserves
the public ABI and all existing events/errors. It does not authorize deployment
or real funds and does not replace remaining proof, release-identity or operator
evidence gates.
