Authority chain
---------------
The checked ProgramBinary is canonically decoded to its ImageID by the
repository proof-program gate. Hosts hash-check the selected program/ImageID,
verify every receipt against that ImageID and exact journal, and require the
pinned RISC Zero Groth16 verifier-parameter digest before publishing a seal.
Adapter initcode embeds that same manifest ImageID; the adapter passes it
immutably to the direct verifier. Compiled initcode/runtime derivation and
reserve/registry config hashes bind the deployed adapter.

The cryptographic verifying-key authority is the reviewed direct RISC Zero
runtime code hash together with the receipt's pinned verifier-parameter digest,
not a replaceable manifest file. `verifyingKeySha256` and the trusted-setup
transcript are release provenance: their content hashes, canonical manifests,
decision report and shared transcript are required by readiness, while proof
acceptance remains pinned to direct verifier code.

Concrete gap fixed
------------------
Finalized-burn and checkpoint manifests point to the same direct verifier and
the same upstream Groth16 setup. Core materialization already required their
direct address/runtime/selector and trusted-setup transcript to match, but did
not require `verifyingKeySha256` equality. Readiness likewise compared only the
three direct-verifier fields at that cross-manifest boundary. Two different VK
provenance artifacts could therefore describe the same proof authority and
still pass those cross-manifest checks.

Production core materialization now rejects differing VK hashes. Mainnet
readiness independently requires both `verifyingKeySha256` and
`trustedSetupTranscriptSha256` to agree across finalized-burn and checkpoint
manifests, in addition to the direct address/runtime/selector.
