ETAPA 1006 — checkpoint candidate selection/finalization — 2026-10-23

Scope
- Fixed target/confirmation heights, cumulative-work comparison, tie-breaks,
  challenge window, replacement/finalize ordering, timestamps, halt and bounds.

Concrete finding and correction
- The comparator used confirmation work then confirmation block, but treated an
  exact rank tie as first-arrival-wins. Selection could therefore depend on the
  relayer/mempool ordering rather than a strict deterministic order. A sound
  ancestry proof normally makes divergent statements sharing one confirmation
  block impossible, but the registry boundary should remain deterministic and
  independent of that extra assumption.
- `_candidateIsBetter` now defines a strict total order: greatest confirmation
  total difficulty, then smallest confirmation block hash, then smallest full
  statement hash. The final key binds every proved field and removes arrival
  order from the remaining tie.
- A regression submits the higher statement hash first, replaces it with the
  lower hash at identical work/block rank, proves readyAt is unchanged, then
  proves the losing candidate cannot replace it back.

Audit conclusions
- Every candidate targets exactly latestHeight+interval and one fixed
  confirmationDepth; constructor and admission arithmetic reject uint64
  overflow. Target and confirmation work must strictly increase.
- Replacement is permitted only before readyAt and never resets readyAt. At the
  boundary timestamp replacement is closed and permissionless finalization is
  open, removing an overlap race.
- The verifier statement authenticates predecessor, target, context, work,
  confirmation block/work/timestamp, deployment/config and activation domain.
  Future confirmation timestamps are rejected before verification.
- Halt is checked before submission and finalization. Guardian/verifier drift
  preserves the candidate for retry but prevents finalization.
