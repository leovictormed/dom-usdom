Acceptance rule

Risk acceptance cannot override a failed consensus invariant, a known path to
unauthorized mint or payout, mismatched artifacts, missing production keys,
unrehearsed control-plane recovery, or a failed mainnet preflight. Those are
technical/configuration failures. This report applies only after both release
gates are otherwise satisfied and every referenced artifact is hash-addressed.

R1 — USDT issuer control

USDT's issuer can pause, deprecate, enable fees, blacklist accounts or make
issuer-authorized balance changes. A reserve or recipient freeze can delay or
prevent payout even when the USDOM burn is valid. Contract checks fail closed
before admission/execution where observable, exact transfer deltas reject fee or
redirection semantics, and renewable queue leases preserve unpaid claims. The
protocol cannot remove issuer authority over USDT.

R2 — irreversible burns and destination mistakes

Native private-note nullifiers make a burn irreversible. Queue expiry no longer
forfeits the claim, but an incorrect recipient, lost recipient key, permanently
frozen destination or permanent reserve freeze can still make value inaccessible.
Wallet confirmation, address checks, recent finalized proofs and renewable queue
leases reduce operational loss; they cannot recover a deliberately immutable
recipient without adding a new, separately reviewed recovery protocol.

R3 — Ethereum consensus and execution

Release safety assumes Ethereum finalized headers, execution payloads, receipts,
storage proofs and the deployed EVM semantics are verified correctly. Consensus
failure, a finalized-chain safety failure or a defect in the embedded verifier
can invalidate that assumption. The system pins chain/genesis identity, bounded
witness schemas, verifier artifacts and current finality and tests reorganization
and ancestry rejection.

R4 — DOM PoW and checkpoint finality

DOM validity still depends on its consensus rules, RandomX relation, cumulative
work and the additional Ethereum checkpoint mechanism. Network partition,
censorship, insufficient honest work, challenge-window failure or unavailable
proof producers can halt checkpoints; a consensus/proof defect can select an
invalid state. Fixed predecessor/context bindings, highest-work competition,
challenge delay, canonical fork floors and permanent guardian halt reduce this
risk but do not make it zero.

R5 — zero-knowledge proof systems

Soundness depends on the native privacy circuit, RISC Zero guest programs,
Groth16/STARK implementations, verification keys, setup parameters and recursive
composition. Cryptographic breaks, incorrect constraints or artifact substitution
could permit invalid state or payout. Exact ImageIDs, code/config hashes,
reproducible source manifests, differential vectors, negative tests and immutable
adapters constrain the release identity.

R6 — privacy and correlation

DOM hides native note values, senders and recipients under the implemented
proof scheme, but Ethereum deposits and payouts expose amounts, addresses and
timing. External observation, amount matching, wallet/network metadata and user
behavior can correlate activity. The release must not claim absolute anonymity
or Monero equivalence.

R7 — wallet keys, backups and rollback

Loss or compromise of viewing/spending material can cause permanent loss or
privacy disclosure. Encrypted backups and snapshot seals do not replace a
production OS/hardware keystore, a rollback-resistant monotonic seal authority
or a rehearsed recovery ceremony. Those integrations remain hard real-funds
requirements and cannot be waived by this report.

R8 — governance, guardian and operational keys

Compromised control-plane keys can pause service or authorize resume; guardian
or governance unavailability can prolong an incident. They cannot directly sweep
collateral or replace immutable verifier/limits, and mainnet starts paused, but
key custody, independent signers, monitoring and rehearsed recovery remain
necessary. Permanent halt deliberately favors safety over liveness.

R9 — congestion, capacity and data availability

Ethereum fees, DOM congestion, RPC censorship, checkpoint/prover outages,
rate-limit saturation and unavailable witnesses can delay issuance or payout.
Delays and queue leases protect review and accounting but do not guarantee a
fixed completion time. Operators must maintain independent data sources and
published liveness objectives without promising continuous availability.

R10 — immutable implementation and migration

The production reserve has no upgrade, sweep or arbitrary-call path. Immutability
reduces administrative attack surface but means a newly discovered defect may
require pausing, deploying a separately reviewed successor and a public migration
procedure. Some failure modes can strand collateral until USDT or the original
recipient becomes usable.

R11 — compiler, build and supply chain

Correctness assumes the pinned Solidity/Rust/Node/Foundry/RISC Zero toolchains,
dependencies and build hosts produce the reviewed artifacts. Source hashes,
locked dependencies, deterministic manifests, source-verified bytecode derivation,
signed commits/tags and independent rebuild evidence reduce substitution risk.

R12 — undiscovered defects and model limits

Passing unit, fuzz, invariant, integration, fork, adversarial and reproducibility
checks cannot prove the absence of every defect. Models may omit rare interactions
or future ecosystem behavior. Monitoring, conservative initial limits, staged
activation, emergency response and explicit exposure caps bound impact.
